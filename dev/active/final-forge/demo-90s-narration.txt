Government bid teams often lose before writing begins. They chase tenders they cannot credibly win, then draft without following the buyer's score.

BidPilot starts with the pursuit decision. This supplier clears eligibility, capacity, and comparable-delivery gates, so the result is PURSUE. Other supplier and tender combinations produce REVIEW and NO-GO.

Next, the official evaluation matrix becomes the control plane. The forty-point technical criterion leads the response, linked to specific supplier assets and an accountable owner.

The agent compares three distinct Win Positions. It selects Proven Data Quality Operations because the recorded delivery history best matches the buyer's highest-weighted need.

That choice drives the proposal. Eight persisted section fragments are assembled beneath four score-bearing criteria. The draft remains editable, passes the live score-map review, and downloads from the public Bid Room.

The red team challenges the most valuable claims. Missing evidence becomes owned work instead of hidden prose. Twelve tasks cover five delivery owners, four adversarial checks, and three provenance controls.

This is not a prompt wrapper. Snowpark executes the pursuit policy beside governed data. Cortex Code writes strategies, plans, sections, and tasks. Snowflake reloads the completed run through a least-privilege reader, with session and query provenance.

BidPilot answers one commercial question from decision to delivery: should we bid, and if so, how do we win the score. Every pursuit becomes reusable team memory for the next bid.
