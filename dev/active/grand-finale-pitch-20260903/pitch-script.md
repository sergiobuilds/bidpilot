## 2 Timed script

### 2.1 Slide 1 · 0:00–0:43

Good afternoon. I’m Sergio Lee, a Washington State CPA who works with government grants and public programs, and a three-time AI hackathon winner. That work taught me a simple discipline: verify eligibility before committing resources, separate source facts from assumptions, bind score-bearing claims to evidence, and give every unresolved gap an owner. BidPilot brings that discipline to public-sector pursuit. It helps a proposal team decide whether to bid, decide how to win the score, and turn that decision into accountable work.

### 2.2 Slide 2 · 0:43–1:25

Proposal teams rarely fail because they cannot produce paragraphs. They fail earlier. A promising tender arrives. Eligibility is checked in one meeting, delivery evidence sits in another system, the evaluation weights disappear into a spreadsheet, and writing starts before anyone has a defensible position. The result is wasted pursuit effort, generic prose, and important evidence gaps discovered too late. The recurring problem is the break between qualification, score strategy, evidence, and ownership.

### 2.3 Slide 3 · 1:25–2:05

BidPilot keeps those decisions in one accountable chain. A public tender and a company’s operating evidence produce PURSUE, REVIEW, or NO-GO. A supported pursuit moves into a score-weighted Win Position, an evidence-bound proposal, red-team review, owned closure work, and the same recorded run in Snowflake. A general LLM can summarize a document and draft fluent text. It does not provide this policy boundary, governed supplier memory, official-weight control, durable run identity, or owned evidence-gap workflow.

[Return to slide 7. Pause 2 seconds.]

### 2.7 Slide 7 · 5:47–6:52

Snowflake is the operating memory for this workflow. The Opportunity Graph joins versioned tender requirements with controlled supplier credentials, availability, people, and delivery history. Snowpark executes the pursuit policy beside that governed data. Cortex Code CLI reads the same graph and writes strategies, plans, proposal sections, tasks, and provenance under one run identity. Streamlit reloads only complete results through the reader role. The runner can create execution artifacts; the reader cannot. If authenticated reading fails, the product fails closed instead of quietly showing a local fixture.

This is why Snowflake is structural. Remove it, and the shared evidence model, policy execution, least-privilege replay, and organizational memory collapse back into disconnected files and chat transcripts.

### 2.8 Slide 8 · 6:52–7:42

The historical run is concrete: one decision; three materially different strategies, with one selected; four weighted response plans; eight proposal sections; and twelve owned and review tasks. Cortex Code CLI queried the opportunity and supplier evidence, created those downstream artifacts, and persisted session and query references. Snowflake then lets the team inspect the result later by the same run ID. The strongest measurable proof today is this complete replayable artifact chain, not a customer outcome or a claimed win-rate improvement.

### 2.9 Slide 9 · 7:42–8:45

BidPilot prepares and governs pursuit work. A human still reviews the source, confirms supplier evidence, owns pricing, edits and approves the final proposal, and performs the legal submission. That boundary matters because public procurement combines formal eligibility, commercial judgment, and accountable representations.

The first buyer is a small B2G proposal team. The commercial path is a team subscription for governed supplier memory, with usage-based runs for intake, decision, strategy, drafting, and review. The immediate value is qualitative and practical: stop unsupported pursuits earlier, focus authors on the score-bearing work, expose risk before submission, and preserve reusable evidence and lessons for the next bid.

### 2.10 Slide 10 · 8:45–9:32

BidPilot directly answers the three evaluation questions. For real-world relevance, it starts with a real public tender and the decision proposal teams must make before writing. For technical execution, Snowpark, Cortex Code CLI, least-privilege roles, and same-run provenance are in the working path. For solution completeness, the verified replay connects decision, strategy, proposal, red-team, ownership, and readback.

BidPilot turns a public tender and a company’s operating data into a defensible pursuit decision, a score-weighted win strategy, and execution-ready proposal work, all persisted and replayable in Snowflake.
